Portfolio case study · How this product was designed

Problem: Product teams often debate priorities without a consistent way to compare opportunities. Spreadsheet-based scoring can become difficult to audit, filter, or communicate.

Users: Product managers, product leaders, and cross-functional roadmap planning teams.

MVP hypothesis: A transparent, editable scoring workspace can make prioritization discussions faster and more structured.

Scope: CRUD feature backlog, validated RICE calculation, live rankings, filtering, local persistence, and CSV import/export. Authentication and real integrations are intentionally out of scope.

Success measures to test:

  • Time required to score and rank five proposed initiatives
  • Percentage of users who can explain the score of the top-ranked initiative
  • Number of data-entry errors detected before saving

Known limitations: Scores depend on subjective estimates, this demo has no shared workspace or backend, and data is stored only on the current browser/device.

Version 2: RICE Coach adds guided onboarding, transparent explanations, step-by-step scoring, and an optional link to a private feedback collection form. A user reviews all suggested entries before saving. Future opportunities: Evidence/source links, stakeholder review, and scenario-based planning.

Portfolio note: This is a demonstration product. The data and outcomes are illustrative; no real user research or business impact is claimed.

How RICE scoring works

RICE gives each initiative a comparable score using four inputs. Higher scores suggest more potential value for the estimated effort, assuming consistent units and estimates.

(Reach × Impact × Confidence) ÷ Effort
Confidence is converted from a percentage to a decimal before calculation. Example: 80% → 0.8.
ReachUsers affected per quarter
Impact0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, or 3
Confidence0–100% estimate certainty
EffortPerson-months, greater than 0

Product judgment still matters

RICE can reduce arbitrary ranking, but a high score does not automatically mean a feature should ship first. Check strategic alignment, technical dependencies, risk, compliance, and estimate quality.

Example: A mandatory security fix might rank below a convenience feature by RICE but still take priority due to compliance or operational risk.

Use this workspace to create a shared starting point for a prioritization conversation—not to replace judgment.
